## What was asked
Verbatim: "in relation to iagl_pwa inverse curve pricing treatment how can i estimate the
impact on bookings volume of the intervention"

Refers to the August 2026 conversion-rate-flip experiment on the Pay-with-Avios (PWA)
product (see [[2026-08-conversion-rate-flip-experiment]]): for the first two weeks of
August 2026, the discount-tier -> conversion-rate mapping was inverted (max discount tier
became most favourable, instead of least) for bookings with a 2026 departure date.

## Verdict

full:

> Adjustment set / paths. The graph gives one confound path from `booking_lead_time` into
> `pwa_bookings` (direct edge, `measured_at: pre_treatment`), and the treatment is a
> deterministic step function of that same running variable. That is exactly the
> sharp-RDD setup, and it is correctly *not* solved by conditioning — RDD identifies off
> local comparison at the cutoff, not adjustment. `pwa_discount_tier_chosen` is correctly
> excluded as a post-treatment mediator/collider, and the instruction not to condition on
> it is right.
>
> `member_price_sensitivity` reasoning is correct, but incompletely checked. It is not a
> confounder of *this* treatment→outcome path, because the graph draws no edge from it
> into `booking_lead_time`. But nobody has asked whether price-sensitive members
> systematically book with different lead times as a stable trait (pre-existing sorting,
> not manipulation). If that edge exists, it does not break the RDD as long as it varies
> smoothly through the cutoff — this collapses into the continuity/manipulation check
> below, not a separate confounding problem.
>
> Continuity assumption is not adequately handled by naming it in prose. A direct edge
> flagged "confound the RDD must neutralise locally" is not itself a solution. The DAG
> notebook must run, at minimum: (a) covariate-smoothness checks around the cutoff, (b) a
> placebo-cutoff check away from 2027-01-01, (c) sensitivity to bandwidth choice.
>
> Manipulation/bunching — analyst's answer is necessary, not sufficient. It rules out
> strategic manipulation in response to the flip; it does not substitute for a formal
> density check (McCrary or a modern analogue) on the running variable at the cutoff.
> Package-holiday bunching near year-boundaries (New Year getaways, school-term

## Design proposed (interviewer, pending reviewer)
**Regression discontinuity on booking lead time (departure date), cutoff 2027-01-01,
within the fixed Aug 2026 flip window.** Not the same-period-last-year DiD — that control
is known contaminated (see [[2026-08-avios-conversion-flip]]) and is not reused here.

Why RDD over the coarse year-boundary DiD the prior attempt considered: the assignment
rule is a deterministic, sharp threshold on a continuous running variable
([[booking_lead_time]]) — every booking with departure ≤ 2026-12-31 gets the flipped
schedule, every booking with departure ≥ 2027-01-01 does not, for bookings all made in the
same two-week window. That is close to the textbook RDD setup, and it lets us compare
bookings just either side of the cutoff rather than the whole 2026 vs 2027+ populations
(which likely differ in trip-planning-horizon composition, e.g. package-holiday bookers
far in advance skew differently from near-term bookers).

What it does **not** get for free: continuity of potential booking-propensity across the
cutoff is an assumption, not a given — [[booking_lead_time]] is left with a direct edge
into [[pwa_bookings]] precisely because departure timing may correlate with
seasonality/urgency independent of treatment. The DAG notebook needs to probe this (e.g.
covariate/density continuity checks around the cutoff) before anything is trusted.

Also flagged: [[pwa_discount_tier_chosen]] is a post-treatment mediator (shaped by
[[member_price_sensitivity]], unobserved) and must not be conditioned on when estimating
the schedule's effect on bookings — doing so risks a collider-induced bias.

`data_mode: simulated` — this design will first be tested on data generated from this
graph with a planted effect, to check the RDD estimator recovers it under a deliberately
non-continuous-looking confound on lead time. That is a code/estimator check, not a
finding about IAGL.
